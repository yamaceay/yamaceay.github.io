<h4>Learn with me one of the most elegant functional programming languages, ReasonML (in less than 20 hours)</h4><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*ikIFHKI0rUxQxzWMM7QECQ.png" /><figcaption>Source: Spoilt for Choice — Purpleslate</figcaption></figure><p>In the huge world of coding, where the number of programming languages, libraries, and frameworks seems endless, finding your path can feel like being a kid in a candy store — overwhelming yet exciting. My journey through computer science had me dabbling in at least ten different languages, each with its own scene and set of challenges. There were moments I found myself wondering whether to stick with the familiar or hit the refresh button and start anew with something different.</p><p>But here’s the real talk: neither the specific language nor the tool you’re learning isn’t the endgame. What matters more is the logic and thought process behind it — the art of problem-solving, understanding the why behind a language’s design, recognizing patterns, and the ability to abstract and break down complex concepts. And most importantly, standing on the shoulders of giants and learning from others.</p><p>After going through my fair share of trials, errors, and breakthroughs, I’ve distilled my experience into a roadmap that might just save you from some detours.</p><h4>Phase 1: “Am I Really Doing This?”</h4><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*sm2WOS5vIH0d0x7euWvvjQ.png" /><figcaption>Source: 6 things to START doing, primalperks.com</figcaption></figure><p>This phase is all about making the call — do you dive deeper into a language or not? If you decide it’s worth the effort, you’re already on the right track.</p><ul><li>In the last few months, as I dove into the world of lambda calculus, type systems, and functional programming, I felt the itch to pick up a new programming language. Haskell? A bit too complex and low-level for my taste right now. So, I leaned towards the ML family, and that’s where I found ReasonML. It’s got the roots in OCaml, which is cool, but ReasonML takes it up a notch. It’s sleeker, more approachable, and just clicked with what I wanted — a functional programming language that doesn’t feel like you’re wrestling with the compiler. ReasonML stood out as the perfect mix of powerful and pragmatic, exactly what I was looking for.</li></ul><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*mHleTfBrKYCalKKXu25xLQ.png" /><figcaption>Source: reasonml.github.io</figcaption></figure><p>Think of this as setting up your base camp. Gather every resource you can find: official documentation, forums, code snippets, and tutorials. Organize them neatly, and make it easily accessible.</p><ul><li><a href="https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&amp;source=web&amp;rct=j&amp;opi=89978449&amp;url=https://www.youtube.com/watch%3Fv%3DsjWsAYJF8BA&amp;ved=2ahUKEwi6hMGwwJKFAxXs6wIHHeRpAh8QtwJ6BAgWEAI&amp;usg=AOvVaw3phZYrJ9e2tWjlKXEORzb5">Bootcamp</a>: Full kickstart course on ReasonML, I’d recommend it personally</li><li><a href="https://reasonml.github.io">GitHub page</a>: Refer to Docs for official documentation and language features, and to API for a complete list of classes / methods along with finer technical details.</li></ul><p>Getting instant feedback is a must for learning from mistakes, so do your setup right. If applicable, just pick the quickest or easiest alternative.</p><ul><li><a href="https://sketch.sh">Sketch.sh</a>: Online notebooks with instant feedback</li></ul><p>This preparation phase is crucial for making your learning process as smooth as possible.</p><h4>Phase 2: “Skim, Skip, Scan”</h4><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*vExSdAmrQoHCjU_0EdbZMQ.png" /><figcaption>Source: Rapid Learning Concept. Brain training and speed reading for best achievement in school or college by Sangoiri, stock.adobe.com</figcaption></figure><p>Next up, it’s time to scout the terrain. Dive into your collection of resources, but keep it breezy. Speed-watch tutorials at 1.5x pace, skip the snooze parts, and focus on what genuinely sparks your interest. If you hit a wall of text, let AI summarize it for you. The same method applies for video transcripts of disturbingly long videos. This phase is about getting the lay of the land, figuring out what’s ahead without getting drown in the details.</p><ul><li>Keep in mind that striking the balance of using AI tools is very crucial. Use too much, you may find yourself merely following along. On the flip side, use too few, you may feel overwhelmed by the unnecessary details. Look for the sweet spot to harness the power of AI effectively.</li></ul><h4>Phase 3: “In The Zone”</h4><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*ORuUp778O-71pGTw2RNLhQ.png" /><figcaption>Source: Flow Theory, A Deeper Dive into Flow States, teuxdeux.com</figcaption></figure><p>Now, you’re ready to dive deep. This is where things start clicking, where you find yourself actually enjoying the process of unraveling the mysteries of a new language.</p><ul><li>One of the aspects I truly appreciate about ReasonML, is how it strikes a sweet balance in coding philosophy. Unlike Go, mandating a uniform style and set of conventions, ReasonML encourages developers to craft their own unique best practices. Yet, it doesn’t throw you into the abyss of choices or give developer complete creative control while sacrificing its own structure, like Python does. For instance, the impossibility of converting a map of strings to a record might seem limiting at first, but I’ve grown to appreciate these constraints. They guide developers towards cleaner, more maintainable code structures without stifling creativity.</li><li>The language’s stance on compile-time error catching, type inference and extensive pattern matching is a game-changer for me. It’s incredibly satisfying to have the compiler intelligently fill in the gaps without being hand-held through every type declaration — a stark contrast to the sometimes tedious explicitness required in TypeScript. This isn’t just about convenience; it’s about efficiency and focusing on the logic that matters.</li><li>It’s not merely a “bookshelf” language, good for theoretical musings; ReasonML proves itself a practical tool for real-world applications. Modules allow for a level of organization and modularity that feels both natural and powerful, and bring the best of Object-Oriented Programming.</li></ul><p>It’s about soaking up the knowledge, piecing together the puzzle, and appreciating the nuances of coding. There’s no rush here; let your curiosity lead the way until you feel that confident nod inside saying, “Yeah, I got this.”</p><h4>Phase 4: “Masterclass”</h4><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*m_i7bhSAY7HvkGyGN8-FnA.png" /><figcaption>Source: To Evolve Is To Create Something Extra, joyebike.com</figcaption></figure><p>This is where you bring it all home. It’s tempting to think you’re done once you’ve grasped the basics, but mastery comes from applying what you’ve learned — tinkering, breaking things, and putting them back together, but better. Start with something that works, then twist and turn it, adding your own flair. This phase is about experimenting, iterating, and refining your approach to coding.</p><p>If you’re not big on starting from the ground up, there’s no shame in leveraging high-level functionalities and working your way down, just do it. It’s a smart strategy that pays off. Take it from me, I’ve fallen into the trap of procrastination and skipped this part, and ended up wasting so much time re-learning the same stuff. Don’t let this happen to you.</p><p>In the following articles, I dove into a few use cases I came up with during phase 4, each of which helped me level up my programming skills on ReasonML:</p><p><a href="https://yamaceay.medium.com/learn-with-me-reasonml-part-1-83b48d9ef694">Learn With Me: ReasonML, Part 1</a></p><p><a href="https://yamaceay.medium.com/learn-with-me-reasonml-part-2-6f704e0ded0b">Learn With Me: ReasonML, Part 2</a></p><p><a href="https://yamaceay.medium.com/learn-with-me-reasonml-part-3-fa246cc034f1">Learn With Me: ReasonML, Part 3</a></p><p>So there you have it. The journey through coding is as much about the process as it is about the outcome. With this approach, you’re not just learning to code; you’re learning to think and solve problems like a developer. Keep it chill, stay curious, and happy coding!</p>
