<h4>Why some ideals are pre-destined to fail, and some are not.</h4><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*UVB93dKJwk9xFIvpQfvTHw.jpeg" /><figcaption>I have an idea, Source: <a href="https://foroalfa.org/en/articles/some-ideas-are-too-big">https://foroalfa.org/en/articles/some-ideas-are-too-big</a></figcaption></figure><p><em>Note: The political ideas below do not reflect my personal opinions and are solely used for illustration purposes. In fact, this article is inspired by a chat with a good friend of mine, Demre, while we were enjoying a few beers in a random pub. Demre is deeply passionate about some ideas discussed here, and our conversation sparked my curiosity to delve into the statistical side of his arguments.</em></p><p>In many cases, the majority of people are unwilling to adopt new life-changing trends unless there is a strong incentive or others <strong>do it</strong> as well.</p><p>For example, people <strong>still</strong>:</p><ul><li>Drive oil-powered cars <strong>instead of</strong> using electric cars or public transportation.</li><li>Consume meat <strong>even when</strong> many alternatives and supplements exist.</li><li>Vote for popular political parties <strong>even after</strong> exploring more appropriate options.</li></ul><p>The <em>all-or-nothing</em> situations are inherently unpredictable: If others did as well, we’d be in a better place. If I did and others didn’t, I’d be the loser. But if none does, we just call it a day and the world suffers <strong>later</strong>.</p><p>A disturbing truth is that the world does not consistently improve, despite our aspirations and regardless of how <strong>flawless</strong> the ideas are. Mostly, it ends up in our <em>default settings</em>.</p><p>So the question arises: <strong>Why</strong> do good ideals extinct and vanish from the world and <strong>who</strong> bears the responsibility?</p><h4>Change By Default, Hopefully</h4><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*vsA0B8ZB8iJjS_Q7G9vv7w.jpeg" /><figcaption>Action vs. Inaction, Source: <a href="https://stock.adobe.com/de/images/action-and-inaction-as-different-choices-in-life-pictured-as-words-action-inaction-on-a-road-to-symbolize-making-decision-and-picking-either-action-or-inaction-as-an-option-3d-illustration/371871147">https://stock.adobe.com/de/images/action-and-inaction-as-different-choices-in-life-pictured-as-words-action-inaction-on-a-road-to-symbolize-making-decision-and-picking-either-action-or-inaction-as-an-option-3d-illustration/371871147</a></figcaption></figure><p>The nature is ever-evolving, dynamic and fluid, as well as <strong>our nature</strong>.</p><p>We often perceive ourselves as static creatures, yet we are constantly navigating an <em>equilibrium of adversarial thoughts</em>.</p><p>One week, we might embrace a vegan diet, only to indulge in a BBQ grill party the next. One day, we contemplate rebellion against the government and capitalism; the next, we focus solely on pursuing our personal goals and financial dreams.</p><p>We are not mere <em>pre-programmed </em>robots. We are self-governing agents, who apply <em>mixed strategies</em> in real-life.</p><h4>Vegans, Game Theory and Statistics</h4><p>This insight allows us to view the world through the lens of <em>game theory</em>. By the way: Have you read my previous article?</p><p><a href="https://yamaceay.medium.com/game-theory-for-dummies-433bc319f7e5">Game Theory For Dummies</a></p><p>Before modeling the world, let’s make a few assumptions to simplify the following calculations:</p><ol><li>Each individual’s choices resemble a probabilistic <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/social-sciences/markov-chain#:~:text=A%20Markov%20chain%20is%20a,Geography%20%28Second%20Edition%29%2C%202009">Markov Chain</a>, where past choices do not (significantly) influence future decisions.</li><li>Everyone has an equal likelihood of changing their mind. Then, the possibility that at least k of n people switch to a new idea is explained using a <a href="https://www.statisticshowto.com/probability-and-statistics/binomial-theorem/binomial-distribution-formula/">binomial distribution.</a></li><li>Imagine scaling the number of people n to millions or billions — it has to be approximated by <a href="https://www.khanacademy.org/math/statistics-probability/modeling-distributions-of-data/normal-distributions-library/a/normal-distributions-review">normal distribution</a>.</li></ol><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*eGx3lx_Q7mTbVWb0ysDBXg.png" /><figcaption>Calculation of success probability of a revolution</figcaption></figure><p>Now, let’s discuss the power of the masses in a very simple exercise:</p><ul><li>For example, at any given day, there’s a <strong>20%</strong> probability of someone adopting a vegan mindset and an <strong>80%</strong> chance of reverting, akin to rolling a dice every day.</li><li>To spark a revolution, we must establish a threshold. Consider that <strong>k = 32</strong> people can create a sensation in an alternate universe of <strong>n = 100</strong> people.</li><li>If, say, <strong>32</strong> people out of <strong>100</strong> can trigger change, the probability of at least <strong>32</strong> people adopting veganism is <strong>0.0013</strong> (with <strong>µ = 20, σ = 4</strong>). In real-life scenarios, these probabilities can become exceedingly small.</li><li>However, if this experiment takes long enough (inverse of <strong>0.0013 ≈ 770</strong> days), we may be sure that the revolution would happen sometime.</li></ul><h4>No Exit From Current Situation?</h4><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*Vhywn0GNKTXoCuCQbWG-qg.jpeg" /><figcaption>Locked in, Source: <a href="https://www.orilliamatters.com/around-ontario/covid-19-pandemic-exposes-how-little-we-know-about-prison-conditions-globally-3534424">https://www.orilliamatters.com/around-ontario/covid-19-pandemic-exposes-how-little-we-know-about-prison-conditions-globally-3534424</a></figcaption></figure><p>Now, let’s approach this from a <em>frequentist</em> perspective: as an event occurs more frequently, the sample becomes a better representation of the underlying population, leading to more accurate estimates in the long run (see <a href="https://www.investopedia.com/terms/c/central_limit_theorem.asp#:~:text=According%20to%20the%20central%20limit,distribution%20is%20normal%20or%20aberrant.">Central Limit Theorem</a>). As the threshold grows, the probability of change diminishes, and people tend to stick with the current <a href="https://www.investopedia.com/terms/n/nash-equilibrium.asp">Nash equilibrium</a> rather than embracing new <a href="https://www.tutor2u.net/economics/reference/pareto-efficiency-and-pareto-improvements#:~:text=What%20is%20Pareto%20efficiency%3F,make%20another%20person%20worse%20off.">Pareto-efficient</a> ideas.</p><p>These daily mental shifts often go <strong>unnoticed</strong>, unless expressed outwardly and taken action. This is why we find ourselves <strong>trapped</strong> in suboptimal real-life situations, and why revolutionary idealistic thoughts often <strong>wither away</strong>.</p><p>So, how can we bring about significant macro changes?</p><h4>Then How to Influence Masses?</h4><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*GkiFSeq5XDY9c7frJY_iJA.jpeg" /><figcaption>Majority vs. Minority, Source: <a href="https://medium.com/@masoodhasan/predicaments-of-the-minorities-and-majorities-78ec8ac916a0">https://medium.com/@masoodhasan/predicaments-of-the-minorities-and-majorities-78ec8ac916a0</a></figcaption></figure><p>Recall from above the probability formula of a <em>massive switch</em> happening. To maximize that, we have two options: (1) decrease the threshold k or (2) increase the switching probability p. Below are 3 possible ideas to get you closer to the finish line:</p><p><strong>#1: Micro-Decisions and Atomic Habits</strong></p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*M3doP6qfS8elRz2_DTJpYw.jpeg" /><figcaption>Tiny changes instead of breakthroughs, Source: <a href="https://www.centercode.com/blog/how-delta-testing-uses-atomic-habits-to-promote-data-driven-decisions">https://www.centercode.com/blog/how-delta-testing-uses-atomic-habits-to-promote-data-driven-decisions</a></figcaption></figure><p>Instead of <em>making personal sacrifices</em> or dedicating your life to a cause, consider making small, gradual adjustments. These <em>nano-changes</em> require fewer people and are less affected by the central limit theorem.</p><p>For example, if you’re a student on a tight budget, donating thousands of euros to a well-known charity may not be <strong>feasible</strong>, but helping someone in urgent need can make a small, meaningful difference, while letting you stay financially stable.</p><p>Akin to <a href="https://fs.blog/the-butterfly-effect/#:~:text=No%20act%20is%20without%20consequences,cause%20a%20tornado%20in%20Texas.">Butterfly Effect</a>: don’t force someone to commit a life-changing decision, recommend <strong>tiny</strong> challenges instead.</p><p><strong>#2: Finding The Common Ground</strong></p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*cqxiws6FT4SZKkavn4qGJg.jpeg" /><figcaption>Public voice, Source: <a href="https://www.inc.com/wee/how-privilege-can-compromise-a-culture-of-inclusion.html">https://www.inc.com/wee/how-privilege-can-compromise-a-culture-of-inclusion.html</a></figcaption></figure><p>Ideas which <strong>include</strong> more people, are more likely to be widely accepted than the extreme ones.</p><p>Let’s say that a group of people wants to attract more people on passing a new environment-friendly law for reducing the CO2 emission. Protesting against all car drivers on streets and burning cars might not be a good option, since it breeds hatred and anger among car drivers towards the environmentalist group.</p><p>Calling for <strong>more cooperative</strong> actions and including larger groups of people on their side could make the real difference. The further the people feel their wishes and needs are met, the higher the increase in probability that they <strong>embrace</strong> an idea.</p><p><strong>#3: Regular Exposure</strong></p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*2cAtuvNNcShFvrOfOrbWjg.jpeg" /><figcaption>Improvement is good, constant improvement is better, Source: <a href="https://luxafor.com/dont-break-the-chain-explained/">https://luxafor.com/dont-break-the-chain-explained/</a></figcaption></figure><p>In real life, we encounter compelling narratives and charismatic leaders advocating various causes. We may feel <strong>inspired</strong> to take action but often <strong>forget</strong> these convictions the next day. What if we could sustain our passion?</p><p>Suppose that certain events, like vegan workshops or art exhibitions about animal cruelty, could increase the likelihood of embracing the vegan lifestyle. If we <strong>remind</strong> ourselves (and the others) of the newly embraced ideas, we can maintain this boost longer and escape the curse of <a href="https://www.hpe.com/us/en/what-is/swarm-intelligence.html">swarm intelligence</a>.</p><p>Similarly, if you face your deepest fears more often, you will become <a href="https://www.physio-pedia.com/Desensitization#:~:text=Desensitization%20is%20a%20treatment%20technique,body%27s%20response%20to%20particular%20sensations.">desensitized</a> to them and soon they will <strong>fade away</strong>.</p><h4>Conclusion</h4><p>In conclusion, this article offers a perspective that encourages continuous evolution and increased exposure to the changes we wish to see. Evolution and dynamicity are inherent to our nature and help us drive innovation more effectively than revolution and dogmatic ideals.</p><p>As we aspire for a better future, we must apply these principles in our personal lives. This is what distinguishes between a goal and a wish.</p><p>Remember, we are all actors playing our roles, and it doesn’t matter whether our roles are big or small. What truly matters is whether we play them correctly.</p><h4>References</h4><p>[1] “7 Ways Oil And Gas Drilling Is Bad For The Environment” by The Wilderness Society. <a href="https://www.wilderness.org/articles/blog/7-ways-oil-and-gas-drilling-bad-environment">https://www.wilderness.org/articles/blog/7-ways-oil-and-gas-drilling-bad-environment</a><br/>[2] “8 Promising Alternatives to Oil” by the Greener Ideal. <a href="https://greenerideal.com/news/energy/promising-alternatives-to-oil/">https://greenerideal.com/news/energy/promising-alternatives-to-oil</a><br/>[3] “Environmental Benefits of Public Transit” by KCATA. <a href="https://www.kcata.org/about_kcata/entries/environmental_benefits_of_public_transit">https://www.kcata.org/about_kcata/entries/environmental_benefits_of_public_transit</a><br/>[4] “Why Is Eating Meat Bad For the Environment and Climate Change?” by Rachel Graham. <a href="https://sentientmedia.org/why-is-eating-meat-bad-for-the-environment/">https://sentientmedia.org/why-is-eating-meat-bad-for-the-environment/</a><br/>[5] “Vegan Meat Substitutes” by Adrienne Seitz. <a href="https://www.healthline.com/nutrition/vegan-meat-substitutes#TOC_TITLE_HDR_4">https://www.healthline.com/nutrition/vegan-meat-substitutes</a></p>
