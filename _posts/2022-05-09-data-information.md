<h4>… and getting information from data</h4><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*3q66GOl6SNBoIhi6n-OGTQ.jpeg" /><figcaption>Source: <a href="https://medium.com/the-coffeelicious/surprising-downside-of-being-smart-16f2fe904122">https://medium.com/the-coffeelicious/surprising-downside-of-being-smart-16f2fe904122</a></figcaption></figure><p>How do you define being smart? What does it mean at all?</p><p>Is it the size of data you store in your brain?</p><p>Is it your book count? Is it your final grade?</p><p>Or is it combining different concepts? Finding new patterns? Being open to challenges and positive change?</p><p>Is it accepting you don’t know anything? Is it rejecting that you already know?</p><p>Answering philosophical questions is not my thing, I guess, so I’ll cut to chase.</p><h4>The More Facts You Know, The Smarter You Get?</h4><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*OJWsNwoSdb4G_6Q6Q9YtVA.png" /><figcaption>Source: <a href="https://preview.btelligent.com/themen/big-data/">https://preview.btelligent.com/themen/big-data/</a></figcaption></figure><p>In my opinion, knowing a lot of facts doesn’t necessarily make you smarter.</p><p>Being smart has a direct relationship with being able to transfer your knowledge from field A to field B.</p><p>So if you are smart, you can also figure it on your own, how to find the fact #16, given facts #1, …, #15.</p><p>But it gets interesting: If you in fact learn more facts, you are more likely to recognize hidden patterns, which makes you more likely smarter.</p><p>But it is time-consuming to take in so many facts.</p><p>What if we had the option to not grok everything but still find the patterns?</p><p>So we find out that most of the textbook facts are boring and underrated. Instead, it is cool to learn how to learn better.</p><p>After graduating from an university, you won’t be using every piece of information that you learned in weeks before graduation exam.</p><p>You will rather remember how you learned, which book you have read to get to the mindset you once had.</p><p>So, with this in mind, can we safely assume that someone who learns how to learn faster becomes smarter?</p><p>Who cares if you know so much facts? People are not driven by facts.</p><p>Wars didn’t happen just because someone realized a fact.</p><p>Sometimes it is more than facts that creates an illusion, it is information.</p><h4>Does Information Drive The World?</h4><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*fxAGaW3IA1KN5rg9t02yXA.jpeg" /><figcaption>Source: <a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datei:Circle-information.svg">https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datei:Circle-information.svg</a></figcaption></figure><p><a href="https://www.diffen.com/difference/Data_vs_Information">Diffen.com</a> defines data and information as follows:</p><p><em>Data</em>: Data is raw, unorganized facts that need to be processed. Data can be something simple and seemingly random and useless until it is organized.</p><p><em>Information</em>: When data is processed, organized, structured or presented in a given context so as to make it useful, it is called information.</p><p>It is okay to know so much about thoughts of Ancient Greek philosophers, but studying philosophers doesn’t make you a philosopher. Likewise knowing what someone smarter knows doesn’t make you smarter.</p><p>Yeah data is the new gold. But information is diamond.</p><p>We are not machines storing ones and zeros. We are processing human bits.</p><p>Information is relevant for us. Information makes us smarter. Context makes us smarter.</p><h4>How to Get More Information?</h4><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1400/1*4Wm4ESzA-kbHSIH07tMHQA.jpeg" /><figcaption>Source: <a href="https://bitnine.net/blog-useful-information/5-ways-to-make-your-brain-smarter/?ckattempt=1">https://bitnine.net/blog-useful-information/5-ways-to-make-your-brain-smarter/?ckattempt=1</a></figcaption></figure><p>So what do we need?</p><p>We need to gain more information from data.</p><p>But how?</p><p>Using statistics, advanced math, machine learning…</p><p>Statisticians are not always liars. Statistics is not always used for manipulation. Statistics is not the art of lying.</p><p>It finds a compromise between people, rather than trying to find the correct answer. It finds the most correct answer.</p><p>So what do you think, who decides which is the most correct answer?</p><p>I tried to answer this question in my <a href="https://medium.com/thedevproject/how-understanding-tradeoffs-made-me-realize-99f2747052b8">other article</a>. Thank you for reading!</p>
